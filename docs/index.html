Repetitions

A repetition measured again, on the same machine with the same C++ compiler, options, libraries and basket, exactly the same generated code, under other commits or at another time. For every series shown: the geometric mean over the programs of the time of the repetition divided by the time of the first measure, and of its ratio to the reference in cpp divided by the first one, with the program that moved most. They say how far two measures of the same code lie apart.

How to read the charts

Every time, in nanoseconds per frame, is divided by the centre of its program: the geometric mean of the reference in cpp and in ocpp, on a logarithmic axis; below 1, faster than the centre. The reference is a level line, solid for cpp, dashed for ocpp; a compared version is a filled square for cpp, a filled circle for ocpp; several versions follow each other in a column, each a little lighter. The colour says the C++ compiler. A thin grey line marks, in each column, the fastest value of the campaign, every series and compiler included, shown or not.

The centre decides what compares. With same compiler or whole machine, each column is divided by the reference of its own compiler or machine: two machines then compare in gain over their own reference, not in speed. With one compiler, every column is divided by the same nanoseconds, those of the reference of the chosen compiler: machines and compilers compare in absolute time. Lock keeps those nanoseconds when the campaign changes, so that every campaign reads in the same unit. A click on a mark pins it: the pin keeps its time and is placed, in any campaign, at that time divided by the centre of its column there (under one compiler, in any column; otherwise only where its compiler was measured); a click on the pin removes it. Two sessions of one machine differ by about 0.2 % in geometric mean and by a few percent on one program, and two campaigns with other libraries compile slightly different programs: a gap of one or two percent between campaigns says nothing. A session that measured the same code again (the same machine, compiler, options, libraries, basket and generated code, whatever the commits) is a repetition: it joins the campaign of the first measure, the charts leave it out unless Repetitions is ticked (hollow marks, thin lines), and its gaps to the first measure are in the table Repetitions. Pins and the lock are kept in this browser. Data: faust-measures, table/measures.csv.
